Method and sources

What each readout means

Slope

Slope in degrees at the 5 m pixel containing the point, from the LOLA product-78 site DEM (Barker et al. 2021). The ± is one published RMS slope error for that pixel. Three regions have no 5 m tile and show no slope; the 20 m mosaic is not substituted. Where the LOLA return count at the pixel is 0, the slope is interpolated.

Sunlight

18.6-year average fraction of hourly steps with any part of the Sun above the horizon, from the 75°S illumination map (Mazarico et al. 2011, 120 m pixel, nearest pixel to the point). It is a long-term average, not the sunlight in a particular week, and has no published error bar.

Earth view

Same map family and averaging period: the 18.6-year average fraction of hours with any part of Earth’s disc above the horizon. Disc visibility, not a DSN link budget. For NR101 only, the hourly timeline on its card is a separate 14-day product, and its window percentages are labelled as such.

Cold ground

Distance from the point to the nearest permanent-shadow pixel (120 m) whose Diviner summer-maximum temperature is below (Williams et al. 2019, 240 m pixel). Searched within 40 km beyond the nine points. The ± is one Diviner pixel, a resolution bound, not a statistical error. Proximity only; no ice-volume estimate.

What is computed in this build

All nine points (first Wueller et al. 2026 Table A1 point per region) are read by the repository’s python -m atlas.regions and exported by python -m atlas.export_web to web/data/regions.json. Every row uses the same measures: slope and RMS error at the point, 18.6-year sunlight and Earth averages, Diviner summer-maximum temperature, permanent-shadow flag, distance to cold shadow, and LOLA return count. Slope, slope error and return count are not computed for Peak near Cabeus B, Mons Mouton Plateau and Slater Plain because product 78 has no 5 m site DEM containing those points. The hourly Sun/Earth series (2025-01-01, 14 and 30 Earth days, 1 m and 5 m) exists for Nobile Rim 1 only, because it needs the Site 06 elevation raster; the other eight cards say so. The home Moon is the LOLA average-sunlight map; its moving light wash only marks the real DE421 Sun azimuth of the open region and is not re-rendered terrain. On each card the Sun azimuth and elevation come from the DE421 ephemeris for all nine points; the site zoom is a real hillshade of the 5 m Site 06 elevation raster for Nobile Rim 1 only, and a static LOLA average-sunlight crop for the other eight (no elevation raster has been processed for them).
