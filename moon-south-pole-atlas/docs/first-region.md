# First region: Nobile Rim 1

NASA’s current Artemis III candidate regions, in no priority order, from the [28 October 2024 update](https://www.nasa.gov/news-release/nasa-provides-update-on-artemis-iii-moon-landing-regions/):

1. Peak near Cabeus B
2. Haworth
3. Malapert Massif
4. Mons Mouton Plateau
5. Mons Mouton
6. **Nobile Rim 1**
7. Nobile Rim 2
8. de Gerlache Rim 2
9. Slater Plain

The 2022 list had thirteen regions. The 2024 list dropped several, including the Shackleton rim sites. NASA has said eliminated sites failed the combination of science, launch window, terrain, Earth communication, and lighting for this mission, and that Shackleton remains a candidate for a later one. This atlas follows the nine, and does not start on a site the current mission has set aside.

## Why this one first

- It is on the nine.
- The LOLA team already published 5 m/pixel elevation, slope, and slope-uncertainty maps for it, under the older name **Site 06** (Barker et al. 2021; [PGDA product 78](https://pgda.gsfc.nasa.gov/products/78)).
- It sits next to two other regions on the nine, Mons Mouton and Mons Mouton Plateau (the feature formerly called Leibnitz Beta). A first map can grow sideways without a new archive.
- Nobile Rim 2 is the natural second region. A multi-criteria study of the older thirteen regions ranked locations inside Nobile Rim 2 highest on visibility, illumination, Earth line-of-sight, and geology (Peña-Asensio et al., Acta Astronautica). Comparing Rim 1 and Rim 2 with one rubric is the point of months 5–6.
- It is not the celebrity crater. Shackleton is the picture everyone has seen. Nobile is where the unglamorous constraints actually get argued.

## Where it is

Nobile crater is centered near 85.3°S, 53.3°E. The candidate region is the rim, not the crater floor.

Representative landing sites inside Nobile Rim 1, from the appendix of Wueller et al. (2026, JGR Planets, [10.1029/2025JE009434](https://doi.org/10.1029/2025JE009434)), cluster around **85.0°S to 85.5°S, 36.8°E to 37.9°E**. Those are study points, not NASA’s official outline.

The region outline to use is the USGS astronaut-navigation grid for the nine down-selected regions: [ScienceBase 10.5066/P1MEQ6UK](https://doi.org/10.5066/P1MEQ6UK), file `Nobile_Rim_1.zip`. Do not trace the region off a press photograph.

An LROC NAC mosaic that covers Nobile Rim 1 together with Mons Mouton spans about 86.1°S–84.8°S and 27.3°E–43.2°E (`NAC_ROI_MTMOUTONLOC`, center −85.4°, 35.3°, 1 m/pixel). That box is a convenient first download window. It is wider than the candidate region.

## Neighbors, in the order we will add them

| Order | Region | Why it is next |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 2 | Nobile Rim 2 | Same crater family. The published “best site” claim lives here. |
| 3 | Mons Mouton | Shares the NAC mosaic and the LOLA site products with Rim 1. |
| 4 | Mons Mouton Plateau | Same massif, different lighting and Earth geometry. |
| 5–9 | Haworth, Malapert Massif, de Gerlache Rim 2, Slater Plain, Peak near Cabeus B | Same rubric, after the method has survived the first four. |

Malapert Massif matters later because Earth stays high there. de Gerlache Rim 2 matters because it is closer to the pole and the nights are harsher. They are checks on the rubric, not the place to discover bugs in the projection.
