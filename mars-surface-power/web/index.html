Method and sources

Real, modelled and illustrative

Modelled · every flux and optical depth

Everything except Opportunity's opacity is Mars Climate Database v6.2 output, read from saved interface responses by the repository's own parser and exported unchanged. Incident solar flux on a horizontal surface at local true solar time 12 h and 0 m above the surface, and the monthly mean dust column visible optical depth. This is not daily energy and not panel output.

Measured · Opportunity 2018 opacity

61 Pancam atmospheric opacity values at 880 nm, sols 5066.54 to 5105.61, Ls 165.6 to 187.9, from PDS product 1tau880_5106_20181207a. Opacity and the database's optical depth are different units. They never share an axis and no conversion factor was applied.

Illustrative · the hero scene

A drawing with real values: sky haze follows 1 − e^(−τ/2) of the real dust optical depth τ; the Sun disc radius follows the square root of the real noon flux against the largest flux in the slice. The horizon, rocks, lander or rover silhouette and the drifting dust motes are decoration. It is not a picture of any site or storm.

Illustrative · replay and strip

The replay steps through 12 seasonal samples; there is nothing between them and no interpolation. 1× is 1.6 seconds per sample. The Mars strip is stylised; only the marker positions are real latitudes and longitudes.

How to read it

The deficit is the stored dusty flux minus the stored cold (clear, low-dust) flux at the same site and Ls: the dusty (warm) scenario and the dust-storm scenario against the cold baseline. Climatology is stored and shown but is not turned into a deficit. The dust storm is the interface's "average" scenario, not a specific year, and exists only for Ls 180 to 330; earlier requests are rejected by the interface, so those samples are absent, never zero.
