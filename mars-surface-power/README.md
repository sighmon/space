# Mars surface power

Not started. The lunar atlas is first.

## Question

Where on Mars is solar power an operational nuisance, and where would a crew be unsafe if solar were their only source through a dust storm?

NASA’s 2024 surface-power decision selected fission for crew-critical loads because suspended dust, not just dust on the panels, can cut sunlight for weeks. Opportunity’s last contact, in June 2018, is the case this map has to be able to show: optical depth rising to about 4.9 within a few sols. The map is about that failure mode. It is not a lander brochure.

## Public records already in the way

| Source | What it holds | Limit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Mars Climate Database](https://www-mars.lmd.jussieu.fr/) | Solar flux, density, and winds on a seasonal and diurnal grid, including dust-scenario settings. Used by published habitat-power studies. | A model climatology validated against observations, not a forecast for a named sol. |
| Curiosity REMS and Perseverance MEDA | Surface pressure, temperature, and opacity from the ground. | Two sites. They anchor the map; they do not cover it. |
| Orbital dust catalogs | Including the OMEGA / Mars Express catalog of hundreds of storms across several Martian years. | Storm tracks and seasons. Predicting which regional storm becomes global is still open. |

Published power studies already integrate the Climate Database for a six-person outpost (for example the Frontiers in Astronomy and Space Sciences photovoltaics paper, 2022, and later hybrid solar-wind work). This project does not repeat their mass budgets. It asks a narrower question: at each map cell, how deep and how long is the sunlight deficit in the dusty scenarios the database already ships, and which landed missions have measured a deficit that large?

## Shape, when this folder becomes active

1. Freeze one Climate Database version and the dust scenarios it documents.
2. Map the deficit relative to a clear-air baseline, in the database’s own units, for a short list of sites that already appear in the human-landing-site literature plus the two rover weather stations.
3. Put Opportunity’s 2018 opacity on the same scale as a check, not as a decoration.

## Standing constraint

Crew-critical power is a flight-program decision already argued by NASA. This map shows the sunlight record those decisions cite. It does not propose a power system.
